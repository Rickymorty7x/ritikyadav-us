Training my first transformer from scratch

Deep Learning Transformers PyTorch

There's a strange comfort in re-reading a paper. You highlight things, nod along, and feel like you've understood. Then you open a blank file and try to write the model from scratch — and suddenly every line is a question.

I spent a weekend doing exactly that with a small character-level transformer, on a single A4000 GPU, training on a chunk of The Complete Works of Shakespeare.

What was easy

The math is well-trodden. Multi-head attention is just a matrix product with some careful reshaping. The encoder block is a stack of obvious primitives. PyTorch's nn.Linear and F.softmax do the boring parts for you.

What broke me

Three things, in order of pain:

  • Tokenization. I started with byte-pair encoding because it sounded cool and lost a full evening to off-by-ones in my vocab. Switched to a tiny character vocab and shipped a working baseline in 30 minutes.
  • Positional encodings. Sinusoidal vs learned vs RoPE — every choice felt religious. I went with sinusoidal because the original paper did, and immediately wished I'd picked learned for how easy it makes ablations.
  • Causal masking. One wrong unsqueeze and your model is cheating; loss drops too fast and you feel briefly amazing.

The first real overfit

The moment your tiny model overfits a 5,000-token slice of text and starts generating quasi-Shakespearean nonsense — that's the moment you finally get the architecture. It's not the diagrams or the equations. It's the warm satisfaction of watching your own implementation produce "To be, or not to be, that is the question of mine" at 4am.

If you're reading a paper and feel like you understand it, write the code. The gap is where the lessons live.

Next up: scaling this to a tiny GPT-style decoder and trying KV-caching from scratch. I'll write that up too.

Story

The longer version of how I got here.

One scroll through the moments that shaped me — from a curious small-town kid to building things with neural networks. Tap any year to expand the story.

Timeline images are thematic stock photos from Unsplash for illustration (not personal archives).

  1. 2005

    The earliest memories are blurry — but family stories agree that I was the kid who'd sit alone with a cardboard box and ask, with absolute seriousness, "how does this fold?" for an hour.

    Curiosity isn't something I learned. It's just where I started.

  2. 2010

    Anything with screws was fair game. My parents tolerated the trail of disassembled electronics across the floor mostly because — most of the time — I'd put them back together. Mostly.

    This was the beginning of a feeling I still chase: the small, quiet thrill of understanding how something works.

  3. 2014

    School was unremarkable until I met a math teacher who treated equations like puzzles instead of formulas. Suddenly math wasn't a chore — it was a quiet, beautiful language for describing things I couldn't otherwise express.

    I started staying back after class to ask "but why does this work?". The patience of that one teacher rewired me.

  4. 2017

    I followed a free Python tutorial late at night and wrote a tiny number-guessing game. It worked. Twenty minutes later I was staring at a blinking cursor wondering what to make next.

    This is the moment I think of as the real beginning. Code felt like a superpower I'd been quietly waiting for.

  5. 2019

    The first neural network I built was a slow, badly-implemented MNIST classifier. 40 minutes on a CPU. 88% accuracy. I remember staring at the terminal as the loss curve descended and thinking, "this thing is actually learning".

    That moment is hard to describe to someone who hasn't felt it. It's part magic trick, part quiet proof that the universe is, in some small way, kind.

  6. 2021

    fast.ai unlocked the speed-of-thinking. Andrej Karpathy's "Spelled-out intro to neural networks" series unlocked the depth. I watched the latter three times in a row, each pass discovering something I'd missed.

    I burned through papers I didn't fully understand and then, eventually, papers I did.

  7. 2023

    I'd read Attention is All You Need four times before actually sitting down to implement it. The gap between those two states turns out to be most of the learning.

    The full write-up lives over on the blog.

  8. 2024

    I started shipping side projects publicly — RAG apps, tiny CLI tools, a couple of fine-tuning experiments. Some got used by exactly one person; some, more. Both kinds taught me something.

    Open source turned out to be the best feedback loop I've ever had.

  9. 2025

    I started writing about ML — not to teach, but to think properly. Putting half-formed ideas into sentences exposes the parts you don't actually understand.

    The collection is small but growing. Read the blog →

  10. 2026

    Currently obsessing over: small language models, agentic workflows, and the messy plumbing that turns a working notebook into something a person can actually use.

    I want to build systems that are useful in the unsexy, durable sense — tools that help people read faster, learn deeper, decide better.

    If any of that resonates, say hi. I'm always up for a conversation about ML, weird side projects, or which paper you've re-read four times without ever implementing.
